Step 2 · Learn the technique

Steps and strategies to help you reframe unhelpful thoughts

Work through these 6 cards to learn each step. Take your time — there's no rush.

Card 1 of 6
01
Know what to look for
We're often not aware we're thinking unhelpfully — which makes these thoughts hard to catch. Knowing the common types of unhelpful thoughts makes them easier to spot.
02
Practise catching them
Try to keep these categories in mind throughout your day. When you notice an unhelpful thought, check whether it fits one of them. This takes practice — but it becomes more natural over time.
03
Check your unhelpful thoughts
Once you've caught an unhelpful thought, step back and examine the situation. Rather than immediately accepting the thought, take a moment to look at the evidence.
04
Change the thought
Now, see if you can replace the unhelpful thought with a neutral or more realistic one. Think back over the evidence you gathered and reframe the situation.
05
Use a thought record
A thought record is a short structured exercise using 7 prompts. It helps you examine evidence for your thoughts and feelings, and begin reframing them. Writing things down makes the process more effective.
06
Don't worry if it's hard
Sometimes you won't be able to change a thought — and that's okay. There are no right or wrong answers. Even just identifying an unhelpful thought is progress. Be patient and kind with yourself.
Step 3 · Practice

My thought record

Think of a situation you've had recently that made you feel worried, anxious or upset. Work through the 7 steps honestly — take your time.

~5 minutes

Thought Record

NHS Every Mind Matters · CBT Self-Help Exercise

1
The situation — what happened?
Describe the situation that triggered these thoughts or feelings
ExampleI am annoyed at myself for forgetting to run an errand.
2
My feelings — how did this make you feel at first?
Describe how you felt in the moment
ExampleFrustrated, stupid, useless.
3
Unhelpful thoughts I had
What went through your mind? Write the thoughts as they were
ExampleI never get anything right. I cannot be trusted with simple tasks.
4
Evidence to support my unhelpful thoughts
What facts seem to back up these thoughts?
ExampleIt's not the first time I've forgotten something like this.
5
Evidence against my unhelpful thoughts
What facts contradict these thoughts? What am I ignoring?
ExampleEveryone forgets things from time to time. I remembered everything else I needed to do.
6
Alternative, neutral or more realistic thoughts
Based on the evidence, how could you think about this differently?
ExampleI remember to do far more errands than I forget. Most of the time I am trustworthy and reliable.
7
How I feel now — after completing my thought record
Has anything shifted? Even small changes are worth noting
ExampleCalmer, more confident in myself. Accepting that sometimes forgetting things is perfectly normal.
